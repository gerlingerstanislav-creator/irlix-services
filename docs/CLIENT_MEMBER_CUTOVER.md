# ClientMember cutover and rollback guide

This guide records the implemented Clients/Timesheets contract and a safe operator sequence for moving to it. It describes code and synthetic checks in this branch; it does not claim that a production migration or reconciliation has run.

## Target model

`client_members` is the durable identity of one person for one client. Exactly one of `employee_id` and `partner_specialist_id` is set. A person working for two clients has two client memberships. Employee IDs come from Employees; partner specialist IDs come from Clients. Numeric IDs from legacy databases are separate namespaces and are never evidence that two people are the same.

Commercial terms belong to the ClientMember. `member_terms.project_member_id` remains nullable historical lineage, while `member_terms.client_member_id` points at the canonical owner. `project_assignments` separately records a member's dated association with a project. A project is optional for a canonical Timesheets fact: its ownership comes from `client_member_id`, and the canonical write stores `project_id = null`. The Timesheets database keeps the legacy project key for old project-scoped facts; its source check requires a fact to be either project-scoped or ClientMember-scoped, never both. No project assignment is inferred from the dates of a commercial rate. The old `ProjectMember` row proves past participation in that project, but does not establish exact assignment dates.

Partner identities are real ClientMembers and are included in internal membership and Timesheets management/reporting. The current interface is an operator workflow guarded by Timesheets management permissions; it does not provide partner login or partner self-service. Any future partner self-service requires an explicit authentication, authorization, and API design.

## Reporting freeze, revision, and snapshot

When Clients advances a reporting period from `Новый` to `ТШ на согласовании`, it asks Timesheets to acquire a durable freeze for the period. Timesheets takes its transaction-scoped reporting gate, validates the reporting period ID, client and exact date range, computes a revision over the owned hours, final approvals and daily exceptions, then persists a freeze token and revision. While that freeze overlaps a date, Timesheets rejects relevant edits with HTTP 423. There is no TTL that silently unlocks a sent period.

Clients reads the complete Timesheets range using that token. It checks the token-bound revision again and checks that the Clients membership catalog revision did not change during the read. It also requires a unique applicable commercial term for each recorded fact and requires every expected member/month slice to have a fact (an explicit zero-hour fact counts) and final approval, with no in-range approval exceptions. If any check fails, Clients keeps the period in its previous status and releases the newly acquired freeze. On success, Clients stores the accepted response and revision in `reporting_periods.timesheet_snapshot` / `timesheet_revision`; later display and Finance reads use this immutable accepted snapshot. Returning the period to `Новый` clears the saved snapshot/revision and releases the freeze. Other reporting periods remain independently protected.

The membership catalog is served by Clients' trusted internal endpoint with `complete`, `count`, `schema_version`, and a content revision. Timesheets refuses an incomplete, malformed, or unavailable catalog rather than pruning facts. Integration tokens are server-to-server credentials and must not be copied into browser configuration or diagnostics.

## Cross-month approved revenue

For a reporting period that spans months, Finance fetches the complete period before selecting the requested month. For each approved commercial term, it uses the reporting-period approved regular hours plus approved overtime multiplied by the period's stored overtime multiplier. It allocates that approved amount over matching actual daily hours for the full reporting period, in proportion to each day's actual hours, then splits those daily allocations into months. Currency is rounded once for the term over the full period and fractional cents are distributed using a stable largest-remainder rule, so the split amounts reconcile exactly to the approved total. The original daily facts are not changed. Missing source facts, invalid approvals, ambiguous periods, or missing commercial terms are reported as incomplete/error evidence rather than guessed allocations. This is an approved-hours revenue allocation rule, not a rule for changing Timesheets facts.

## Conversion and ambiguity policy

1. Inspect the source and target schema versions and run the canonical Clients and Timesheets dry-runs against read-only legacy access. Take and verify compatible coordinated rollback snapshots for Clients, Timesheets, and Migration's private metadata/state before any target write. Follow the snapshot and restore controls documented in [Migration](MIGRATION.md); do not treat a successful backup command alone as proof that restore works.
2. Migrate Clients before Timesheets. Clients consolidates a person's project rows into one ClientMember per client/person, preserves source term IDs and payloads in lineage, and coalesces only compatible overlapping commercial terms. It does not create synthetic default projects or dated project assignments. Check the resulting membership and term counts and inspect every warning/conflict before continuing.
3. Resolve Timesheets employee identities through Employees mappings. Resolve a source member to a unique target ClientMember using verified identity plus client/project evidence. Partner identity requires an explicit reviewed `timesheets.members` mapping. A legacy client reference requires an explicit `timesheets.clients` mapping; project names or matching numeric IDs alone do not prove identity. Then resolve each source fact date to exactly one target commercial term. All source facts merged into a single ClientMember/day must resolve to the same commercial term; otherwise the group is blocked with `FACT_GROUP_TERM_CONFLICT` instead of silently choosing an arbitrary term. Gaps, overlapping target terms, unsupported identities, over-24-hour person-days, and unverifiable confirmation history block the affected import.
4. Review every manual override against the current source evidence and target ClientMember's person/client. Re-run dry-run after changing overrides. Import only after a clean dependency-plan dry-run, then Validate and reconcile member/day hours, descriptions, zero-hour rows, source IDs, approvals, and mapping lineage. A retry must not duplicate or overwrite target facts changed after the initial import.

The expand migration intentionally preserves legacy project members and term rows. Compatible coalescing stores the original term payload and alias in `member_term_lineage`, retaining existing approval references. Incompatible overlapping terms, protected reporting history that would be altered by coalescing, ambiguous approvals, invalid intervals, or unresolvable identities must stop for explicit reconciliation. Do not pick a winner, rewrite dates, synthesize a project assignment, or discard an approval to make a migration pass. By owner decision (24 Oct 2026), source facts with `rate_id = NULL` are **excluded from this cutover only**, without classifying them as internal activity. Their complete source rows are retained in the original read-only source and the private migration warning evidence (`RATELESS_FACT_EXCLUDED_BY_SCOPE`); the dry-run reports the number excluded, and post-import validation reconciles their source count and exact decimal hours separately from mapped client facts. They must not produce target `timesheet_entries` or mappings. Any pre-existing mapping to an excluded fact blocks validation; the exclusion is not a license to erase a target fact. The known inventory is 76 rows / 320 hours, but every new run checks the live read-only source rather than trusting these counts. The old Timesheets `is_submitted` flag and month-confirmation rows do not prove an auditable actor/time or complete per-connection confirmation; they are not converted into new approvals automatically.

## Safe rollback and schema checks

Do not run Laravel `migrate:rollback` against a populated canonical schema as a cutover rollback. The Clients ClientMember migration's `down()` refuses some client-only terms but then drops canonical membership, project-assignment, and feedback tables; the reporting-snapshot migration's `down()` throws and requires explicit reconciliation. A reverse migration is therefore not a safe data-preserving rollback procedure.

For a cutover rollback, stop writes and reporting transitions, record the deployed revisions, and restore the compatible pre-cutover snapshots of Clients and Timesheets together with Migration's matching private metadata/state and credentials using the approved Migration Ops restore procedure. Verify schema versions, row counts, key relationships, and read-only/health checks before reopening writes. Never drop canonical columns/tables or discard Migration mappings to force services onto mismatched schemas. If the coordinated snapshots or restore checks are unavailable, keep writes stopped and reconcile forward; do not improvise destructive SQL.

At minimum, preflight and post-restore checks should cover:

- Clients: `client_members` identity XOR and client/person uniqueness; `member_terms.client_member_id`, nullable legacy `project_member_id`, and `superseded_by_term_id`; complete `member_term_lineage`; project ownership and `project_assignments` date/overlap constraints; `connection_attempts.client_member_id`; client-member feedback and original project references; reporting-period freeze token, revision, and snapshot columns.
- Timesheets: `timesheet_entries.client_member_id` / nullable `project_id` exclusivity, uniqueness by ClientMember/date, `final_approvals` and exceptions, `timesheet_legacy_sources` lineage, `reporting_freezes`, and audit rows.
- Integration: Clients membership response is complete and count/revision agree; Timesheets can resolve the catalog; a frozen reporting period returns the same snapshot and revision; the linked Client/Timesheets schema revisions are compatible.

## Reproducible synthetic checks

Run these from the repository root with Docker available. Each build executes the service's Dockerfile test stage against synthetic SQLite fixtures and lints the PHP source; it does not contact production or prove production data reconciliation.

```sh

docker build --target app -t irlix-timesheets-client-member-check ./services/timesheets
docker build --target app -t irlix-migration-client-member-check ./services/migration
```

The focused checks are `services/clients/tests/client_members_migrations_smoke.php`, `services/clients/tests/reporting_freeze_smoke.php`, `services/timesheets/tests/runtime_contract_smoke.php`, `services/migration/tests/canonical_clients_import_smoke.php`, `services/migration/tests/client_member_import_smoke.php`, and `services/migration/tests/fact_stage_large_smoke.php`. ## Actual-source rehearsal

The feature's `Client member PostgreSQL validation` workflow also has an owner-authorized `actual-source-copy-dry-run` job on pushes to the feature branch. It executes a fixed SELECT-only exporter in the currently deployed Migration container, without bootstrapping the application or deploying feature code. PostgreSQL reads use enforced read-only transactions; SQLite metadata and identity ledgers are opened read-only. Source and existing target rows, mappings, and the legacy data-decryption key travel over SSH into private temporary files on the ephemeral runner. Connection passwords are not exported.

The runner restores these rows into disposable PostgreSQL databases, applies the canonical populated upgrades, and runs the actual migration dry-runs and project-fact converter. The runtime Docker network is internal and publishes no host ports. Only aggregate counts, conflict codes, schema column names, and fixed safe diagnostics may appear in logs; private rows, keys, raw database errors and container logs must never become artifacts or caches. Temporary files and containers are removed on exit. PR jobs receive no SSH credentials. A pre-import validation result is diagnostic: it cannot establish successful post-import reconciliation. Any blocking dry-run conflict or populated-upgrade failure must be resolved or explicitly reconciled before cutover.

Legacy Timesheets partner member rows lack a reliable corporate UUID; numeric IDs and matching names cannot establish identity. Operator candidate inspection now shows matching ClientMember names, exact client-label evidence, and actual source-fact date coverage. Potential names without proven client are **advisory only** and cannot be submitted as verified connections. Every partner still requires explicit, reviewed `timesheets.members` override to a real partner ClientMember; never synthesize a partner Employee, impersonate another client, or write an automatic mapping solely from a name. The 21 outstanding source partner member rows may include multiple connections for the same specialist; confirm per source row and client. The private Migration UI is the place for the human check; personal names must not be logged to public CI.

## Authorized empty-target cutover (24 Oct 2026)

The owner confirmed the current *new-platform production target* contains no data that needs preservation and authorized discarding it during initial migration. Therefore **do not convert old target project-hour rows or pre-existing final approvals** just to obtain a canonical schema: initialize the target Timesheets state empty instead. This is an initial migration policy, not a runtime permission to bypass `ReportingFreeze` or alter future client-approved history. Source legacy Clients/Timesheets and original Employees identity evidence remain read-only until imported data and mappings have been verified.

The isolated real-source CI rehearsal implements this policy using the guarded `discard-old-target-timesheets` mode, which can execute **only** against its ephemeral `copy-postgres/target_timesheets` database; there is no production reset from CI. Deployment must use an explicitly scoped operator-controlled target reset only after stopping dependent writes, identifying every affected service/database and restoring canonical migrations. Never run a broad `docker compose down -v` or drop Keycloak/server configuration as a substitute. After reset, Clients→Timesheets staged dry-run, partner identity confirmation, import and reconciliation are still required. Rejected rows with NULL legacy `rate_id` remain accounted for as approved exclusions, not deleted from source.

## Read-only source diagnosis (24 Oct 2026)

The isolated, real-source rehearsal on a disposable copy reported: 60,024 source Timesheets facts, 76 explicitly excluded NULL-rate facts (320 hours), 59,948 importable source facts; 21 partner member rows, of which 19 have historical hours, 9 have one exact name+client-label candidate, and 7 also have unique date-compatible ClientMember terms. Twelve partner source members have no exact name+client-label candidate (not proof of absence of a valid real match). Four employee member references also require client-specific review. With previous targets still populated, the initial conversion was correctly blocked by 15 frozen-period facts and one frozen approval; per owner decision, the new isolated rehearsal uses an empty Timesheets target instead. None of these diagnostics authorizes automatically binding partner identities, inventing approvals, or reporting migration success. Later CI runs must verify the new path. Later private-source runs are explicitly gated to a feature-branch `workflow_dispatch` or a push whose commit message contains `[source-dry-run]`; ordinary code commits run synthetic PR checks only, avoiding repeated copies of private sources.

## Owner authorization for autonomous partner reconciliation (24 Oct 2026)

The owner explicitly permits automated matching for all legacy partner participants without interactive confirmation. The importer now automatically maps a partner **only** when there is exactly one canonical ClientMember whose normalized full name (or precisely the same two-or-more full-name tokens in a different order) and legacy client/project label match and whose MemberTerms cover every actual source fact date; the method is recorded in migration lineage. Permission is not evidence for an arbitrary mapping. Partners with zero or several independently plausible candidates, missing fact-date evidence or incompatible periods remain conflicts, not fabricated mappings. Their available evidence remains in the private operator review UI for further automated investigation. This replaces the earlier blanket requirement for interactive approval of uniquely proven identities, but manual review is still supported for cases lacking unique evidence.

## Additional unattended identity diagnostics

When a legacy employee is proven by corporate UUID but the free-text legacy project label no longer matches Clients, automatic selection is permitted only if that employee has **exactly one** current ClientMember across all clients and the matching MemberTerms unambiguously cover every actual historical fact date. Empty or overlapping ranges and employees with multiple client memberships remain unresolved. This fallback never joins legacy numeric IDs, creates new Clients memberships, or supplies fictitious rates; its regression tests explicitly reject absent dates, wrong terms and competing clients. Partner matching remains separate and requires exact name/client/date evidence.

The client/project identity comparison is now exact after folding internal whitespace and case. Partner name-token permutations require every token to match exactly, at least two tokens, a unique person+client candidate and complete fact-date coverage. Initials, partial names, approximate spellings and ambiguous permutations are rejected. Synthetic ClientMember import checks cover labels with repeated internal spaces and reject similar-but-different labels. This release candidate requires a fresh isolated-source dry-run at the final feature SHA.

The 23 Oct read-only aggregate on a disposable source copy narrowed the unresolved partner root cause: 12 of 21 source partners have no exact full-name match in imported Clients partner memberships (0 cases of one exact name but a different client, and 0 ambiguous exact-name matches). Two partners have no sourced Timesheets facts. The new isolated check explicitly tests whether any of those 12 represent an exact permutation of complete name tokens (not a shortened or fuzzy name), while keeping final attribution blocked unless client and dated terms also uniquely agree. Rate-date conflicts (203 fact rows in the latest completed pre-change run) remain independent and must be resolved without inventing terms. This count is diagnostic, not proof of completed migration.

## Private-source root-cause triage

The isolated copy now emits aggregate-only diagnostics after a blocked Timesheets dry-run: counts of unresolved partner vs employee identities, partner exact-name absence, alternate-client candidates, missing source hours, and employees with one date-compatible candidate. The analysis runs against the already loaded private disposable data only. No private names, person identifiers, dates, client identifiers, or source-row contents appear in public CI logs. The same aggregate diagnostic separates historical RATE_DATE_UNRESOLVED conflicts into no effective MemberTerms for a fact date versus overlapping active terms; it never repairs source rates by extending or inventing contract periods. A blocked dry-run continues to fail the CI job; diagnostic output must never be treated as a passed migration or an instruction to force-write an ambiguous match.
