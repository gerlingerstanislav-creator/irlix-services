# Brand platform, positioning and messaging

## Distinctions

- **Category**: what buyers compare us against, not what we aspire to be.
- **Positioning**: who + need/context + frame of reference + distinctive value + credible reasons.
- **Value proposition**: concrete customer outcome/effort/risk reduction; not a technical feature list.
- **Mission**: enduring reason to operate; should describe actual work, not claim to save the world.
- **Vision**: plausible desired future, aspirational and visibly not a current fact.
- **Values**: must translate into repeatable observed behaviours and costs (what we won't do).
- **Slogan/tagline**: optional condensed creative expression, never a substitute for positioning.
- **Brand personality**: consistent communication characteristics; **tone** varies by context (incident, sales, hiring).

## Positioning statement (internal)

"For [specific decision-maker and situation] who [job / anxiety], [brand] is a [credible category] that [differentiated benefit]; unlike [plausible alternative], it [mechanism], evidenced by [specific verified proofs]."

Draft at least two directions with genuine tradeoffs: one might stress engineering reliability, another speed/adaptability or team experience. Don't just rewrite synonyms.

A generic "trusted innovative high-quality partner" fails: no buyer, category, contrast or reason to believe.

## Reason to believe (RTB)

Build claim→proof map:
1. Benefit claimed and segment for whom it matters.
2. Mechanism: operating practice / staff / documented capability / process.
3. Artifact: publishable anonymized case, method, policy, metric, reference, interview.
4. Proof strength and freshness.
5. Permission to publish; owner and refresh date.
6. If absent, change assertion to hypothesis, reduce specificity, or exclude.

Never manufacture results such as "reduces time by 40%". If a claim depends on internal data, do not write that data into public artefacts; seek approved aggregated evidence.

## Message architecture

- Master proposition: one sentence, factual where possible.
- 3 pillars maximum: benefit, why credible, practical example, required proof.
- Variants by audience: buyer, technical reviewer, candidate, internal team.
- Objections: "how do you ensure...", "why not hire internally?", "can you prove..."
- No two pillars may claim mutually inconsistent positioning.
- Avoid copywriting hyperbole "number one", "100% effective", "always" and unapproved security assurances.
- Channel examples are provisional until their claims and rights are approved.

## Tone of voice

Pick 3–4 attributes with **Do / Don't** examples. E.g. "expert but readable" means clear reasoning and concrete nouns, not jargon or aggressive self-praise. Review for inclusive language, clarity, audience and regional context. Reserve slogan approval for the owner; an interesting phrase from an archived file is not automatically active.

## Decision experiment

Present proposed claims without logos to 3–6 representatives of distinct buyer roles (qualitative, no statistical claims), ask comprehension, credibility and recall; compare against 2 alternatives. Measure exact observations (not made-up metrics). Update position after evidence rather than reinforcing prior choice.
