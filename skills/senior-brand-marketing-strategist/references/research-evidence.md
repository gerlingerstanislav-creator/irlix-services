# Research, segmentation and evidence hygiene

## Evidence ledger fields

Use one entry per important assertion, not just a bibliography:
`ID | claim | observation/source | source type | observed date | temporal validity | evidence strength | approved for external use? | conflicting data | next verification`.

Classify *observed* vs *claimed by source* vs *independently verified* separately. An official site is authoritative on what it says, not necessarily on whether a boast is objectively true. Testimonials prove what a quoted client said (subject to rights), not objective superiority.

For current market assessments, record the geographic footprint, publication vs event date and retrieval date. If an inaccessible source, label it inaccessible. No invented interviews, conversion figures, survey samples, TAM/SAM/SOM, competitor pricing or growth trends.

## Segmenting (STP) without invented personas

- **Segmentation**: business outcome, procurement model, risk, organization size and industry constraints (rather than demographics).
- **Targeting**: score each segment 1–5 with *explicit assumptions* for fit, credible differentiation, reachability, capacity, buying complexity, proof and constraints. Scores are decision aids, **not measured market data**.
- **Positioning**: choose a customer-relevant difference, specify the category and sufficient proof.
- **B2B buying committee**: economic buyer, engineering evaluator, procurement/security, day-to-day user; distinguish pains and non-negotiables.
- **Employer brand**: candidates can be a separate audience with a distinct EVP; it may share values, but should not overwrite B2B customer messaging.
- **JTBD format**: "When [situation], I need [job/outcome], so I can [progress], despite [constraints]." Verify through interviews rather than treating invented personas as facts.

## Interviews (qualitative)

Ask about most recent **actual** choice, considered alternatives, triggers, risk, disqualifiers, proof needed, decision process, and what happened; avoid leading "Would you love an innovative provider?" questions. Separate buyer, lost prospect, current customer, candidate and employee samples. Note sample selection, consent, confidentiality and bias. No statistical generalization from five conversations.

## Competitor screen

For 3–6 meaningful alternatives capture: category, verifiable promise, supporting proof, typical buyer, apparent points of parity, potential advantage and uncertainty. Use company public material with date, not fabricated experience. Include in-house teams / do-nothing in B2B. Don't market by insulting rivals or giving unverified claims about their capabilities.

## Claim safety ladder

- Safe as **public-source observation**: "На сайте компания сообщает X" (with date/source), if not restricted.
- Conditional **proposal**: "Мы можем позиционироваться как X, если подтвердим Y".
- Ready for **brand rule** only after owner's approval and proof/right-to-publish assessment.
- Mandatory review: certifications, data residency, import substitution, regulated sectors, security, client logos, customer success metrics, team size, job promises, trademark ownership and support SLAs.

**Escalate:** legal/IP/PD-sensitive or misleading claim risk; mark BLOCKER and ask for responsible clearance before public release.
